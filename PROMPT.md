# Copy-paste carousel prompt

Works in Claude, ChatGPT, Codex or any capable assistant. Copy everything between the lines, fill in the brackets, and attach your source and brand files.

---

Help me make a social media carousel for my business. Use only the tools actually available in this conversation.

**Business and audience:** [what you do, who it's for]
**Source or idea:** [paste or attach the reel, post, article or notes]
**Brand:** [attach logo, colours, fonts or handwriting files, and 2 to 3 posts you like; or say "propose one"]
**Output:** [cover only or full carousel; platform and size if known]
**Ending:** [e.g. comment keyword "GUIDE" for a checklist I've prepared, or "save this"]
**People:** [real photos attached / fictional people OK / no people]

Work in this order:

1. **Understand the source.** Before rewriting, list its hook, memorable wording, examples, numbers (with units and dates), important qualifications and the order things are revealed. Keep what makes the framing work; don't reinvent the angle to sound clever. Mark which points are source facts and which are your hypothetical examples. If you can't open the source, tell me what's missing instead of guessing.

2. **Plan in chat first.** Show a table: slide number, short headline, one short supporting line if needed, key labels, and what the visual will explain. Use plain words a busy business owner understands. Let the story decide how many slides. Wait for my approval.

3. **Design each whole slide together.** Plan the headline, imagery, relevant logos, explanatory objects, labels, arrows and empty space before making anything. Don't put default text on a finished background. If spelling or exact fonts need fixing, add a precise text layer that fits the planned layout. Give interior slides the same care as the cover.

4. **Make the visuals teach.** Use detailed images, real examples, the relevant app screen, comparisons or diagrams. Arrows must connect meaningful things. Every number needs a unit and context. Measured charts need accurate proportions; artistic size comparisons must say "Illustrative comparison". Never invent screens, results or reviews.

5. **Keep text readable.** Short headline, a brief supporting line only where needed, useful labels. Don't put the whole script on the slide; put the fuller story in the caption. Make space by composing, not by deleting essential information.

6. **Respect my brand.** Use my fonts, handwriting, colours and logos consistently. Generated lettering isn't an exact font match; say so if exact fonts matter. When a well-known brand is part of the explanation, show its genuine logo or screen where it matters. Don't copy any example's colours, people, wording or layout unless I ask.

7. **People, if used.** Believable ordinary people doing real work, natural skin texture and a range of skin tones, realistic hands, eyes, teeth and proportions. For Australian briefs, use local settings, buildings, clothing, equipment and language, with a range of people; never treat "Australian" as one ethnicity or guess nationality from appearance. Keep a real person's identity consistent if I supply photos. Fictional people must never appear as staff, customers or testimonials.

8. **Clear ending.** If I asked for a comment keyword, make that exact word stand out with contrast or a highlight, and match the promise to the resource I've actually prepared. Don't invent a download or an automation.

9. **Revisions.** When I change a title, keep the scene, important figures and style. Keep final, alternative and trial versions clearly separated.

10. **Tools, honestly.** Keep the model I chose. Check what tools you really have before promising images. If you can generate images, use that tool. If you can't, say so in one line and give me a complete image prompt for each slide; use my photos or an SVG/HTML layout where that suits. Don't ask me to install paid services or add API keys, and don't switch providers without telling me. Tell me which tool produced each image.

Before you finish, check every slide at full size and at phone size: spelling, numbers, hands and faces, readability, overlapping elements and overall composition. Tell me what you checked and anything you couldn't.

---

Optional: attach images from the `examples` folder as style references and say which one and what you like about it. Treat them as inspiration, not a template.
