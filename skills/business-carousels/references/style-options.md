# Style options

Starting directions, not templates. Pick one in the plan, adapt it to the business's own fonts, colours and logos, and hold it across the deck. Mix nothing that the brand does not support.

| Option | Feels like | Works for | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Photoreal cutaway** | Detailed object opened up to show what's inside | Trades, products, anything with a hidden mechanism | Labels must name real parts; keep the cutaway plausible |
| **Explained diagram** | Clean technical drawing or render with a traced path | Processes, causes, "where it really goes wrong" | Arrows follow the real path; no decorative arrows |
| **Human moment** | Candid documentary photo of the work happening | Services, hospitality, care, consultations | Natural skin, real hands, fictional people never shown as staff or clients |
| **Quiet editorial** | Lots of space, refined serif or light type, soft light | Premium products, beauty, considered services | Small labels can vanish on a phone |
| **Playful collage** | Torn paper, stickers, bright tags | Food, retail, behind-the-scenes stories | Keep the sequence readable; the claim must be true for that business |
| **Sculptural data** | Dimensional objects standing in for numbers | Finance, growth, comparisons | Label "Illustrative comparison" unless sizes are measured |
| **Restrained bold** | Modest heavy headline, handwritten notes beside objects, generous whitespace | Explainers and news | Handwriting must be the real font if it is a brand font |

Typography moves: a single highlighter or brush stroke under the key word, a circled number, or a light headline for a calmer feel. Use one, not all.
