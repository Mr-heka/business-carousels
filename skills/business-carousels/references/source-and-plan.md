# Source and plan

## Pick the mode

- **Repurpose a source** (reel, carousel, article, transcript). The source already shows what works. Keep its angle; adapt the format.
- **Develop an idea.** Anchor on the user's own words, the specific product or subject, and the benefit they want the reader to get. Research only new factual claims the brief needs.
- **Revise approved work.** Change only what was asked. See [CTA and revisions](cta-and-revisions.md).

## Extract before rewriting

Write a short framing record:

1. **Hook:** the exact opening line or image, and where it appears in the source.
2. **Recognition anchors:** the person, product, brand or event that makes it instantly familiar.
3. **Memorable wording:** phrases worth keeping and what each means.
4. **Examples and numbers:** each with its unit, period and source. Note whether it is a source fact or your hypothetical example.
5. **Qualifications:** anything that changes the meaning if dropped ("reported target, not a completed IPO", "includes a one-off accounting charge").
6. **Reveal order:** what the reader learns first, next and last, and where the payoff lands.
7. **Keep / adapt / remove:** default to keep. Valid reasons to change: clarity, format, audience, accuracy. Sounding clever is not a reason.

If the source cannot be opened, plan only from what you could read and name the gap.

## Plan in chat

Show this table before producing anything:

| # | Headline | Supporting line (optional) | Labels | What the visual explains |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Short hook | One line, only if needed | Essential labels | The relationship or evidence the picture teaches |

Then one line each for: chosen style direction, the ending action, and anything unknown.

Rules for the plan:

- Use words an ordinary business owner uses. Translate jargon before naming it.
- Split at real changes in meaning. A demonstration, story, comparison, news update or checklist each has its own shape. There is no default slide count, tips format or problem/agitate bridge.
- Each slide completes one thought and makes the next swipe feel worth it.
- Keep the full script or narration in the caption or notes. The slide carries the headline, a short line and labels, not paragraphs.
- Label hypothetical examples as examples. A creator's reported result is not proof the new author achieved it.
- Wait for approval. Once approved, the wording is locked unless the user changes it.

## Cold-reader test

For each slide ask: what changed, who acts, what does the reader now understand, and what on screen proves it? If the answer depends on the caption, the slide is unfinished.
