# People

Use people when an interaction, task or emotion carries the point. Not every slide needs a person; a mechanism or screen may teach better.

## Casting and setting

- Believable people doing the actual job, not glossy stock models smiling at the camera. Choose age, build, clothing, posture and expression from the business and the moment.
- Ask for natural skin: pores, fine lines, pigmentation variation, flyaway hair, realistic proportions. Avoid plastic smoothing, skin whitening, oversaturation, model-perfect casting and exaggerated weathering.
- Expressions and gaze fit the interaction. Not everyone smiles at the lens.
- Show professional tasks safely and plausibly: correct equipment, no exposed live wiring, no blades near eyes.

## Australian briefs

- Establish place through setting: architecture (weatherboard, Queenslander verandahs, terraces), landscape and light (eucalypts, shade cloth, subtropical sun), equipment, everyday clothing and local language (flat white, footpath, tradie).
- Australia is a place, not one ethnicity. Cast a range of people who reflect the country.
- Never infer anyone's nationality from appearance. Avoid flags, caricature and imported settings (American diners, school buses, European café postcards).

## Real people and fictional people

- **Supplied real person:** keep their identity consistent on every slide (face shape, hairline, skin, age, eye colour). Do not beautify or swap in a lookalike. Use their photos only for the brief they were supplied for.
- **Generated fictional person:** a demonstration only. Never present them as staff, a client, a testimonial, before/after evidence or a result. Do not give them invented names, quotes, reviews or credentials. Say in the caption or notes that the imagery is illustrative when that matters.
- **Children:** side or back views, nobody identifiable, ordinary clothing.

## Text around people

Put text in real clear space. Protect eyes, mouth, hands, the action and the key object. Pointers land on objects, never across faces.

## Check the actual image

At full size: eyes, teeth, ears, fingers and grip, joints, contact points (feet on floor, hands on cups), shadows, mirror reflections, duplicated limbs or objects, and any generated background lettering. At phone size: does the interaction still read, and is the headline legible? Fix concrete defects with a targeted edit. Keep an accepted image unchanged when only wording changes.
