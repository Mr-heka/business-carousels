# Brand and identity

## The business's own brand comes first

- Ask for or inspect: logo files, colours (hex values if known), fonts, any handwriting font, and two or three existing posts the business likes.
- Preserve those consistently across every slide. The bundled examples are possibilities, not a palette or template to copy.
- If the business has no brand yet, propose one direction in a sentence, get approval, then hold it for the whole deck.

## Fonts and handwriting

- Naming a font in an image prompt does not load that font. Generated lettering, including generated handwriting, only approximates it.
- When exact fonts matter, render the final type from the actual font files (HTML, SVG or code) as a finishing layer that follows the planned composition.
- Never describe generated lettering as the brand font unless the glyphs were rendered from that font file.
- Check licences before bundling or sharing font files.

## Recognised brands in the explanation

When a named product, app or company is part of the point (a Xero invoice, a Gmail inbox, a Claude logo on a valuation chart), show its genuine visual identity on the object where it matters:

- Use genuine, inspected logos or screenshots supplied by the user or available through authorised tools. Keep their colours, proportions and lettering.
- Place a mark where the tool acts in the story, not as a logo row or a footer.
- A generated dimensional version of a logo is an editorial recreation; compare it to the real mark and fix distortions.
- A logo identifies an object. It does not prove an integration, partnership or result.
- Never invent a logo, accreditation, award, rating or endorsement for a real or fictional business.
